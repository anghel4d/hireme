# Observer corpus, systems and languages territory: techniques for hireme

Scope: the Broadside Observer pools `systems`, `languages`, `engines`, `realtime` and the applicable part of `maths-foundations`, swept on 2026-10-20 for techniques that make hireme's kernel derivations incremental, its CV rule language statically analyzable, its replica and wire leaner, and its text processing more correct. Card links point at the hosted corpus (`https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/<id>.md`). Code links are relative to this file (`docs/research/`). Agents and LLM topics are covered by a separate researcher and are left out here.

## Which Observer cards describe techniques that could make hireme's derivations incremental, its rule language analyzable, its replica and wire leaner, or its text processing more correct?

### Takeaway

The Observer is strongest exactly where hireme hurts most. Its incremental-computation shelf has DBSP, counting/DRed, Rete/TREAT, Adapton, a 2026 OOPSLA contract for per-cell net-effect deltas, and a 2026 measurement paper on dirty sets. That shelf maps directly onto two measured costs in the current kernel: a 31 ms whole-desk CV pick pass the first time an application is opened after any application write (8 rules, 10k applications), and a 3.7 to 4.3 ms full board re-derivation after every write, including writes no derivation reads. The pattern-matching shelf (Maranget's decision trees, abstract interpretation, Davis–Putnam, Bonsai) supports data-independent rule analysis, though the corpus lacks the usefulness and guard-tree papers themselves. The wire/netcode shelf mostly confirms hireme's design; the actionable leftover is resuming from an older snapshot with a delta (Quake III style). The text shelf covers UAX #29, UAX #9 and minimal finite-state lexicons, but not normalization (NFKC), stemming or CJK indexing.

#### Code facts the measurements trace to

- Any non-empty, non-clock move sets `stale = true`, and the next board read re-derives every card and re-sorts. Nothing checks which table or column moved. — [abi.rs:154-170](../../native/kernel/src/abi.rs#L154), [board.rs:541-597](../../native/kernel/src/board.rs#L541)
- `Moved` records, per table, the written and deleted row ids; it carries no column information. — [rows.rs:563-588](../../native/kernel/src/rows.rs#L563)
- Any application write drops the card cache entry for that id regardless of which columns changed. — [board.rs:456-466](../../native/kernel/src/board.rs#L456)
- Any write to `applications`, `places`, `corporations`, `cv_rules`, `cv_masks`, `cv_roots`, `me` or `languages` drops every CV pick. Opening an application then calls `pick(id)`, which rebuilds `Picks::of` over the whole desk. — [abi.rs:164-167](../../native/kernel/src/abi.rs#L164), [abi.rs:271-274](../../native/kernel/src/abi.rs#L271), [pick.rs:318-322](../../native/kernel/src/pick.rs#L318)
- `pick()` evaluates every enabled arm for every application, pinned ones included, because it collects `also` (all other matching arms). — [pick.rs:101-121](../../native/kernel/src/pick.rs#L101)
- `AppFacts::read` dispatches on the field's path string at every read. `company.size`/`company.tier` and `field`/`subfield`/`seniority` normalize and lexicon-scan the company name and role per application on every pass; the memo is per `AppFacts` instance, not shared with the board's per-distinct-text `Memo`. — [pick.rs:219-232](../../native/kernel/src/pick.rs#L219), [pick.rs:248-310](../../native/kernel/src/pick.rs#L248), [board.rs:131-160](../../native/kernel/src/board.rs#L131)
- The rule editor's `mask_check` reports `shadowed` and `never_matches` only over today's applications: it evaluates the new rule and the earlier arms on every application. — [pick.rs:143-175](../../native/kernel/src/pick.rs#L143)
- The mask language: `|`, `&`, `!`, conjunction lists, named masks, typed tests (`=`, `!=`, ordered comparisons, `in`, `~` word-contains, `^=` prefix), enums ordered by declaration, and absent-field semantics in which a comparison on an absent field is false. — [mask.rs:1-22](../../native/kernel/src/mask.rs#L1)
- Text: `fold` maps only Latin ranges U+00C0..U+024F and U+1E00..U+1EFF to base letters; other scripts keep their marks. ### Inferences

- The four concerns map onto card clusters unevenly. Incremental derivation is well served, with both seminal (Rete 1982, counting/DRed 1993) and current work (DBSP 2023, commit-window contracts 2026, dirty-set measurements 2026). Rule analysis is served only indirectly: the decision-tree paper names the usefulness check but the corpus has no card for it. Replica and wire work is largely validation of hireme's existing prediction-plus-rollback model. Text correctness is thin.
- The biggest measured cost is not the one the brief named ("full re-derivation after a write", about 4 ms). It is the CV pick pass, 31 ms, which lands on "opening an application", one of the interactions held to sub-10 ms. The cause is a cache that is dropped wholesale by writes that cannot change most picks (a `notes` edit triggers it too). That is precisely the failure IVM theory addresses: picks are a row-local map joined to a few small tables, so their delta is row-local.
- The worst-case `DISTINCT` desk misses the documented 30 ms first-derive bound on both the served and HEAD builds under Bun. Chromium/V8 may differ, but this is worth a check by whoever owns the bound.

### Gaps

- No corpus card for: Maranget's usefulness algorithm ("Warnings for pattern matching", JFP 2007) or Lower Your Guards (ICFP 2020); NFKC/UAX #15 normalization, case folding, UAX #31; stemming; CJK bigram/n-gram indexing; inverted indexes and full-text search; roaring bitmaps; lightweight column compression (FOR/PFOR, FastLanes, BtrBlocks, FSST, ALP); CRDTs; Salsa / build-systems-à-la-carte / Jane Street Incremental; FRP and signals; BDDs and model checking; deterministic simulation testing; hash consing. The zero-hit queries above are the evidence.
- WebAssembly as a topic is barely present (topics `webassembly` and `wasm` do not exist; 3 text hits). Nothing in the corpus covers wasm allocators or wasm SIMD specifically.
- Hosted hit counts were not re-measured (coordinator instruction). The local emulation is exact by construction and agreed with the one hosted query, but it was not cross-checked query by query.
- The measurements are from Bun/JavaScriptCore on one machine, not Chromium, and the 8 rules are synthetic; the operator's real rule set may be larger or smaller.

## For each relevant technique: what is it, how strong is the evidence, how recent, is there a Rust or wasm implementation, where does it plug into hireme, and at what cost and risk?

### Takeaway

Ten techniques survive. The first two (row-local, demand-driven picks; read-set dirty filtering) are classic IVM moves with small diffs that remove the measured 31 ms and the 4 ms paid by irrelevant writes. Next come batched bitset evaluation of masks, delta-propagating board derivation, and write-time materialized facts, which attack the remaining full passes and the cold path. Then data-independent rule analysis (Maranget usefulness over typed field domains), delta resume, I-confluence-guided prediction, Unicode-correct segmentation and Myers diff. None needs a new runtime dependency: the corpus supplies the algebra and the evidence, and hireme's kernel already holds the data in the right (columnar) shape.

### Cited Findings

#### C1. Row-local, demand-driven CV picks (IVM delta for a map-plus-join view; demand-driven refresh)

- DBSP defines the incremental version of any stream operator as `Q^Δ = D ∘ Q ∘ I`. The chain rule pushes Δ to the leaves; linear operators incrementalize as themselves, and primitive relational operators have incremental forms faster than recompute by roughly `|R|/|ΔR|`. Tables are Z-sets (insert +1, delete −1). Implementation: a Rust library plus a Calcite SQL-to-DBSP compiler that passed all 7 million SQL Logic Tests, with a Lean formalization. The card warns that benchmarks are deferred to the repo. PVLDB 16 (2023), arXiv 2203.16684, DOI 10.14778/3587136.3587137. — [card 1489](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1489-dbsp-automatic-incremental-view-maintenance-for-rich-query-languages.md), [paper](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol16/p1601-budiu.pdf)
- The DBSP paper won a VLDB 2023 Best Paper award. Feldera's open-source Rust implementation powers its query engine, and a longer version appeared in the VLDB Journal (vol. 34, 2025). — [Feldera blog](https://feldera.com/blog/Best-Research-Paper-VLDB-2023), [Feldera papers page](https://docs.feldera.com/literature/papers/)
- Counting (Gupta–Mumick–Subrahmanian, SIGMOD 1993) keeps, per derived tuple, a count of its alternative derivations. For non-recursive views it computes only tuples actually inserted or deleted; DRed (over-delete, then re-derive) is needed only for recursion. Seminal, still the standard non-recursive answer. — [card 1841](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1841-maintaining-views-incrementally.md), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/170035.170066)
- TREAT (Miranker, AAAI 1987) keeps the conflict set across cycles instead of Rete's β-memories and beats Rete on real OPS5 programs, often by more than 50%. Rete (Forgy 1982) makes each working-memory change cost work proportional to the change. Both are seminal. — [card 1004](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1004-treat-a-better-match-algorithm-for-ai-production-systems.md), [card 042](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/042-rete-a-fast-algorithm-for-the-many-pattern-many-object-patte.md)
- Adapton (PLDI 2014) records a demanded computation graph so only what an observer reads is refreshed, with large wins when demand is sparse. Its own caveat: when everything is demanded anyway, the demanded graph is pure overhead. — [card 1466](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1466-adapton-composable-demand-driven-incremental-computation.md), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/2594291.2594324)
- hireme: picks are dropped on any write to eight tables, and the next application open rebuilds them for the whole desk: 31.2 ms at 10k applications with 8 rules, 0.469 ms with 0 rules, 0.086 ms when cached. — [abi.rs:164-167](../../native/kernel/src/abi.rs#L164), [abi.rs:271-274](../../native/kernel/src/abi.rs#L271), measurements above

#### C2. Read-set dirty filtering: per-table and per-column relevance, net-effect deltas per cell

- Aotani–Kamina (PACMPL 10, OOPSLA 2026, DOI 10.1145/3839467) give reactive ECS runtimes a Rocq-mechanized contract. A net-effect commit emits at most one event per cell per commit window; observers are order-insensitive within a window; `Changed` is a dirty-by-write filter, not a semantic-equality test. The paper proves schedule independence under write-disjointness and correctness lemmas for caching continuous-query membership. It is formal semantics only, with no runtime numbers. — [card 1853](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1853-commit-window-observation-contracts-for-reactive-ecs.md), [SPLASH 2026 page (abstract)](https://2026.splashcon.org/details/oopsla-2026/93/Commit-Window-Observation-Contracts-for-Reactive-Entity-Component-Systems)
- Bevy stores an added tick and a changed tick per component and compares them with each system's `last_run` (`is_added`, `is_changed`), the cheap production form of column-level dirty tracking. An old issue reports missed changes when a 32-bit tick counter wraps. — [card 316](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/316-bevy-ecs-getting-started-design.md), [bevy_ecs change-detection source (mirror)](https://doc.qu1x.dev/bevy_trackball/src/bevy_ecs/change_detection/mod.rs.html), [bevy issue #3071](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/issues/3071)
- Out of the Tar Pit (2006, essay): keep essential state relational and derived state as pure functions. This is the precondition that makes read-set filtering sound in hireme, since the kernel's derivations are pure functions of rows plus `today`. — [card 1821](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1821-out-of-the-tar-pit.md), [lib.rs header](../../native/kernel/src/lib.rs)
- hireme: a `notes`-only application write costs the same 4.26 ms board derive and the same 31.2 ms pick pass as a stage move. A `contacts` write costs a 3.93 ms derive although the board never reads `contacts`. `Moved` carries row ids, not columns. — measurements above, [rows.rs:563-588](../../native/kernel/src/rows.rs#L563)

#### C3. Column-at-a-time (bitset) evaluation of the mask language

- MonetDB/X100 (CIDR 2005): tuple-at-a-time engines spend about 90% of TPC-H Q1 outside the arithmetic. Vectors of about 1000 values with selection vectors run Q1 in 0.50 s against 0.22 s for hand-coded, 1–2 orders of magnitude above the iterator engines. — [card 129](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/129-monetdb-x100-hyper-pipelining-query-execution.md), [paper](https://www.cidrdb.org/cidr2005/papers/P19.pdf)
- Kersten et al. (PVLDB 2018): with shared algorithms, compiled vs vectorized deltas are modest (+74% on Q1, +32% on Q9), and both crush classical interpreters. — [card 833](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/833-everything-you-always-wanted-to-know-about-compiled-and-vect.md), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.14778/3275366.3275370)
- Direct-operable bit-slicing (arXiv 2608.26368, 2026): equality, range, IN and BETWEEN predicates as bitwise operations on bit-planes, 2.4–10.8× faster than scalar scans on 50M TPC-DS-shaped rows. It is Java/Panama-first and author-independent. — [card 1627](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1627-direct-operable-simd-bit-slicing.md), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26368)
- Column-store design (FnT 2013): operate on encoded columns as long as possible, with late materialization. — [card 834](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/834-the-design-and-implementation-of-modern-column-oriented-data.md)
- Lazy code motion (PLDI 1992) makes partial-redundancy elimination four unidirectional bit-vector analyses: hoist what is invariant. Superoperators (POPL 1995) fuse common opcode sequences so an interpreter pays one dispatch for several operations. — [card 2335](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/2335-lazy-code-motion.md), [card 1438](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1438-optimizing-an-ansi-c-interpreter-with-superoperators.md)
- hireme: the pick pass walks each rule's tree once per application through `AppFacts::read`, which dispatches on path strings. Text-derived fields re-normalize per application. `me.*` tests are constant across applications but re-evaluated for each. Measured: first `cv_picks` 51.8 ms; `mask_check` 2.7 ms per call. — [pick.rs:248-310](../../native/kernel/src/pick.rs#L248), [pick.rs:143-175](../../native/kernel/src/pick.rs#L143), measurements above

#### C4. Delta-propagating board derivation

- How the Dirty Set Moves (arXiv 2610.19489, 2026): patching from the changed rows alone is 2.1–8.2× faster than the best of eight full-recompute codes. A cached tiled schedule loses to the plain delta kernel in 779 of 837 workloads, a 2.5× kernel win shrinks to 0.87× once the whole pipeline is charged, and a learned strategy selector costs 39.51 ms of metadata plus 53.95 µs per decision. Its guidelines: always compare against full recompute; default to the plain delta kernel. The authors are a small team, and the corpus is synthetic-heavy. — [card 2328](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/2328-how-the-dirty-set-moves-schedule-reuse-in-incremental-sparse-matrix-multiplication.md), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19489)
- DBSP: group-by aggregates are linear (indexed Z-sets) and joins incrementalize by the bilinear rule, so a change to one group's aggregate re-touches only that group's members. — [card 1489](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1489-dbsp-automatic-incremental-view-maintenance-for-rich-query-languages.md)
- The Fenwick tree (Software: Practice and Experience 1994) gives O(log n) point update and prefix query in a flat array, an order-statistic primitive for rank positions. Seminal. — [card 2254](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/2254-a-new-data-structure-for-cumulative-frequency-tables.md), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/spe.4380240306)
- hireme: the heat index already keeps per-company and per-vendor groups updated per application (`Index::update`). `derive()` still recomputes every card's verdict and re-sorts all keys (a nearly sorted stable sort): 3.86 ms per one-row write at 10k. — [heat.rs:488-611](../../native/kernel/src/heat.rs#L488), [board.rs:541-597](../../native/kernel/src/board.rs#L541), measurements above

#### C5. Write-time materialized per-application facts, versioned like keywords

- Counting/DRed and DBSP are both about keeping a materialized view current at write time instead of recomputing it at read time. — [card 1841](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1841-maintaining-views-incrementally.md), [card 1489](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1489-dbsp-automatic-incremental-view-maintenance-for-rich-query-languages.md)
- C-Store (VLDB 2005) splits a small writeable store from a large read-optimized store (overlapping sorted projections, four compression encodings) under snapshot isolation. — [card 127](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/127-c-store-a-column-oriented-dbms.md)
- TED (AIIDE 2024, game-ai pool): derived tables recomputed every tick cost 7.55 ms against 6.01 ms for hand C# on its benchmark. That is the non-incremental baseline, and it is acceptable only when the tick budget allows it. — [card 1490](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1490-fast-declarative-character-simulation-using-bottom-up-logic-programming.md)
- hireme already stores `keywords` plus `keywords_version` at write time, with a server backfill when extraction changes. The cold derive normalizes and lexicon-scans every distinct company, role and location: 24.94 ms, or 34.05 ms on the all-distinct desk (bound 30 ms). — [rebuild.md "Keywords at write time"](../rebuild.md), [judge.rs:246](../../native/kernel/src/judge.rs#L246), [board.rs:493-537](../../native/kernel/src/board.rs#L493), measurements above

#### C6. Data-independent rule analysis: usefulness over typed field domains, with witnesses

- Maranget (ML Workshop 2008, DOI 10.1145/1411304.1411311) compiles matches through clause matrices. Column "necessity" reduces to the usefulness check OCaml already uses for exhaustiveness and redundancy warnings. Heuristics were measured; on real programs, code size barely changed. — [card 2309](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/2309-compiling-pattern-matching-to-good-decision-trees.md), [preprint](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/~nr/cs257/archive/luc-maranget/jun08.pdf)
- Not in the corpus: Maranget, "Warnings for pattern matching", JFP 17(3), 2007. It defines useless clauses and non-exhaustive matches and gives a simple detection algorithm, shipped in OCaml and usable for Haskell. — [Cambridge Core](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-functional-programming/article/warnings-for-pattern-matching/3165B75113781E2431E3856972940347)
- Not in the corpus: Lower Your Guards (ICFP 2020) reduces matching to guard trees with three constructors and checks coverage compositionally. It is implemented in GHC and is more accurate, and in some cases faster, than GHC's previous checker. It matters here because hireme's masks are boolean guards over typed fields rather than constructor patterns. — [Microsoft Research page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/lower-your-guards-a-compositional-pattern-match-coverage-checker/), [ICFP 2020 page](https://icfp20.sigplan.org/details/icfp-2020-papers/17/Lower-Your-Guards-A-Compositional-Pattern-Match-Coverage-Checker)
- Rust's own checker, `rustc_pattern_analysis`, is Maranget-based, extended for Rust's patterns, and handles integer ranges precisely with `IntRange`; its entry point is `compute_match_usefulness`. — [rustc_pattern_analysis docs](https://doc.rust-lang.org/stable/nightly-rustc/src/rustc_pattern_analysis/lib.rs.html), [IntRange](https://doc.rust-lang.org/stable/nightly-rustc/rustc_pattern_analysis/constructor/struct.IntRange.html)
- Refinement types for ML (PLDI 1991): declared subtypes form finite lattices, and inference is abstract interpretation over them, so spurious exhaustive-match warnings go away. Abstract interpretation (POPL 1977) gives the soundness frame for interval and set domains. — [card 903](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/903-refinement-types-for-ml.md), [card 701](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/701-abstract-interpretation-a-unified-lattice-model-for-static-a.md)
- Davis–Putnam (JACM 1960) decides propositional satisfiability by eliminating variables. Cook (1971): SAT is NP-complete, so general rule redundancy is coNP-hard, which is harmless for rule sets of a few dozen atoms. — [card 804](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/804-a-computing-procedure-for-quantification-theory.md), [card 724](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/724-the-complexity-of-theorem-proving-procedures.md)
- Bonsai (PLDI 2026, arXiv 2511.15000) classifies predicate-against-bounds as always, maybe or prune using symbolic interval analysis, compiling in 1–5 ms. It has the same shape as classifying a rule against the earlier arms. — [card 1037](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1037-bonsai-compiling-queries-to-pruned-tree-traversals.md), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15000)
- hireme: `check` decides "shadowed" and "never matches" only over today's applications. A rule that is shadowed for every possible application is reported as fine on a desk where the shadowing arm happens to miss, and an empty desk reports every rule as never matching. — [pick.rs:143-175](../../native/kernel/src/pick.rs#L143)

#### C7. Delta resume against an older snapshot rev

- Quake III sends snapshots delta-compressed against each client's last acknowledged frame, keeping a sliding `PACKET_BACKUP` window, and discards a snap whose delta base is too old. GPL source, 1999; the default FPS netcode shape. — [card 412](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/412-quake-iii-arena-networking-source.md), [sv_snapshot.c](https://github.com/id-Software/Quake-III-Arena/blob/master/code/server/sv_snapshot.c)
- Client-side prediction with server reconciliation: the server tags state with the last processed input, and the client replays unacknowledged inputs on top. — [card 269](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/269-client-side-prediction-server-reconciliation.md)
- hireme: resume is a PATCH only when `snapshot_rev` equals the current rev; any lag means a full BOOT (≤ 2 MiB packed at 10k). Rows carry a `rev` column, and the kernel exports `snapshot()`/`restore()`. — [sequencer.ex:423-441](../../lib/hireme/sequencer.ex#L423), [rows.rs](../../native/kernel/src/rows.rs), [abi.rs:304-320](../../native/kernel/src/abi.rs#L304)

#### C8. I-confluence classification of commands for optimistic prediction

- Bailis et al. (PVLDB 2014, arXiv 1402.2237): an invariant is I-confluent if any two locally valid states from a common ancestor merge into a valid state. Such operations need no coordination. Uniqueness of a scarce resource and sequential ids fail the test; monotonic counters pass. — [card 845](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/845-coordination-avoidance-in-database-systems.md), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.2237)
- Reconciliation guidance: do not locally kill or mutate irreversible state the server has not confirmed. GGPO predicts, rolls back and resimulates on misprediction. — [card 269](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/269-client-side-prediction-server-reconciliation.md), [card 471](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/471-ggpo-overview-and-design-notes.md)
- hireme: a NACK undoes every pending write, drops that op and reapplies the rest. The heat governor caps concurrent applications per company and per ATS vendor, a shared-resource invariant that is not I-confluent when the operator and agents write concurrently. — [rows.rs header](../../native/kernel/src/rows.rs), [heat.md](../heat.md)

#### C9. Unicode-correct segmentation and folding

- UAX #29 (reviewed at Unicode 18.0.0, Revision 49, 2026-09-01) defines grapheme, word and sentence boundaries as table-driven pair rules. Default word boundaries break around every ideograph, so Japanese, Chinese, Thai or Lao need a dictionary or a tailoring. The card advises pinning the UCD version. — [card 1858](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1858-unicode-text-segmentation-uax-29.md), [UAX #29](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr29/)
- UAX #9 resolves display order for mixed left-to-right and right-to-left text (relevant to the web layer rendering listings and names). — [card 1599](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1599-unicode-bidirectional-algorithm-uax-9.md)
- Daciuk et al. (Computational Linguistics 2000) build the minimal acyclic DFA for a sorted word list in one pass, in O(l log n), with a working set no larger than the final automaton plus one word. Seminal. — [card 2238](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/2238-incremental-construction-of-minimal-acyclic-finite-state-automata.md), [paper](https://aclanthology.org/J00-1002.pdf)
- hireme: folding covers only Latin ranges. Keyword words are maximal runs of letters and digits, so an unspaced CJK sentence becomes one "word". The stop list is English. The most recent commit reads Word files containing CJK and Latin-2 text into CV text. — [text.rs:1-17](../../native/kernel/src/text.rs#L1), [keywords.rs:1-47](../../native/kernel/src/keywords.rs#L1), commit `552e80c` message (git log)

#### C10. Myers diff for CV versions, overlays and listing edits

- Myers (Algorithmica 1986): O(ND) worst-case time, O(N + D²) expected, and a linear-space "middle snake" variant. It underlies GNU diff and git's default diff and ran 2–4× faster than System V's diff. Seminal. Caveat: it minimizes edit count rather than readability. — [card 2354](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/2354-an-ond-difference-algorithm-and-its-variations.md), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01840446)
- hireme: CVs are now edited in place and saved as the next version, while the file stays as uploaded and the text is what rules, coverage and agents read. — commit `552e80c` message (git log); [query.rs header (`history`)](../../native/kernel/src/query.rs)

#### Application notes per candidate (estimates)

- **C1 (do first).** In [abi.rs `moved`](../../native/kernel/src/abi.rs#L154), stop clearing `picks` on `applications` writes. Re-pick only the written ids (`pick::pick(rows, arms, id)` is already per-application). For `places`/`corporations` writes, re-pick the applications that reference them, either through a reverse index or, since such writes are rare, by re-picking all. Keep the full rebuild for `cv_rules`, `cv_masks`, `cv_roots`, `me` and `languages`. Make `application(id)` call `pick::pick` directly when no `Picks` exist (demand-driven) instead of building all. Note that `pick` reads the pin columns on `applications`, so `pick_cv`/`unpin_cv` are row-local too. About +40/−15 lines in `abi.rs`/`pick.rs`; no binary-size change. Risk is low because a pick is a pure function of its row, the referenced place/corporation, `me`, `languages` and the rules. Guard it with a seeded property test "incremental picks = `Picks::of`" beside the existing "base ⊕ deltas = fresh read" law.
- **C2.** Two steps. (a) Table relevance: give each derived cache a constant read-set of tables (the board's is `applications`, `settings`, `me`, `places`, `corporations` and the CV lineage tables) and skip `stale = true` when a move touches none of them. That removes the 3.93 ms on `contacts`, `messages`, `events`, `citations`, and similar writes. (b) Column relevance: have `Moved` record a per-table column bitset (the PATCH already names moved columns: "changed rows (id + moved columns)"), and have each derivation declare the columns it reads. `mask::Expr::fields()` already computes a rule's field set, which maps to columns. A `notes` or `letter_raw` edit then costs nothing derived. About +40/−10 lines. The risk is a missing column in a read-set, which leaves a stale card. Mitigate with a debug assertion comparing against a full derive, as the commit-window paper's "dirty-by-write" framing suggests. Bevy's wraparound issue argues for u64 revs (hireme's revs are already u64).
- **C3.** Evaluate each `Test` once per application into a bitset over the `applications` slot order (10k bits is 157 u64 words). Combine `All`/`Any`/`Not` with word-wise AND/OR/NOT. Arm i's first-match set is `M_i ∧ ¬(M_1 ∨ … ∨ M_{i−1})`, `also` comes from the same bitsets, and `mask_check` becomes one new bitset plus a cached prefix-OR. Resolve field paths to accessor functions at compile time instead of matching path strings per read. Tests over `me.*` only are constant, so evaluate them once into all-ones or all-zeros. Share the board's per-distinct-text `Memo` for `company.size`/`tier`/`field`/`subfield`/`seniority`, or read C5's materialized columns. About +150/−80 lines (the per-application tree walk stays for `explain`). wasm SIMD is unnecessary at 157 words. The risk is a semantics mismatch on absent fields (`!(country = DE)` vs `country != DE`). Mitigate with a differential test: bitset evaluator vs `Expr::matches` on fuzzed desks, a technique the repo already uses with `fuzz.mjs`.
- **C4.** Treat the board as `cards = map(applications)`, `verdict = join(card, load[company], load[vendor])`, `order = sort(key)`. When application *a* changes, recompute *a*'s card. If *a*'s heat contribution changed, recompute verdicts for the members of *a*'s company and vendor groups (the `heat::Index` groups already exist), then delete and reinsert only the changed keys in an ordered set (`BTreeSet<(u128, u32)>`). Positions come from a Fenwick tree over rank, or from re-materializing `order` lazily only when `view` or `ids` is read. Keep the full derive for `settings`/`me`/`places`/`corporations` moves and day changes, and follow the dirty-set paper's G1/G5: keep full recompute as the fallback and charge the whole pipeline when measuring. About +120/−40 lines. The risks are verdict dependencies beyond the two groups (department and clone penalties, `kept` seats) and the fact that a vendor group can hold a large share of the desk, so the worst case stays O(group). Prove it with an "incremental board = fresh derive" property test.
- **C5.** In the sequencer's `judge` on `put` (where keywords are already extracted), compute the application's org/title/place facts and store them as compact enum or bit columns with a `facts_version`, backfilled like `keywords_version`. The board's `cached()` and `AppFacts` then read columns instead of normalizing text. Corporation renames need a join backfill: re-derive all applications of that corporation. Schema lines plus about +50/−40 kernel lines. BOOT grows by a few u32 columns, compressible and byte-shuffled. The risk is lexicon edits needing a version bump plus backfill, the same discipline as keywords.
- **C6.** In `mask.rs`, add an analyzer over `Node`. Atoms are typed tests. Abstract domains: enum value sets (bitsets, 16 or fewer values each), numeric and day intervals (open/closed bounds), finite literal sets with a co-finite "other" for codes and text `=`/`in`, and presence. `~` (word-contains) and `^=` (prefix) stay uninterpreted atoms with a few sound implications, for example `role = "rust engineer"` ⇒ `role ~ "rust"`. Decide `arm_i ∧ ¬(arm_1 ∨ … ∨ arm_{i−1})` by bounded cube enumeration or a small DPLL with per-field consistency checks (Davis–Putnam plus interval and set reasoning, the LYG "oracle" style). Report three verdicts: `shadowed_always` (certain, independent of data), `never_matches_any` (a contradiction such as `score > 80 & score < 50`), and a witness for "an application of this shape reaches no rule and falls to the default". Keep today's data-driven `shadowed` beside them. About +300 lines in a new section of `mask.rs` (net-positive; it needs offsetting deletions under the net-zero rule); +10–30 KB wasm (estimate). Depending on `rustc_pattern_analysis` is not advised: it is a compiler-internal crate, and the algorithm is small. The risk is false "maybe" verdicts where text atoms interact; report those as "possibly shadowed", never as errors.
- **C7.** In the sequencer, keep a ring of recent PATCH bodies keyed by rev, or answer "rows with rev > snapshot_rev plus deletions since" from SQLite. That needs a deletion log or tombstones for hard deletes. Resume with a PATCH whenever `snapshot_rev` is inside the window, and fall back to BOOT as Quake III discards stale baselines. Elixir only, about +60/−5 lines; no wasm change. The risks are grant changes between snapshot and resume (a BYE `grants` already exists) and schema changes (the HELLO already zeroes `snapshot_rev` on a `schema_rev` mismatch).
- **C8.** Let the schema compiler emit a per-command `confluent` flag: an op is confluent when its judge checks are row-local and monotone (notes, stage moves without a cap, citations), and non-confluent when it touches shared caps (heat), uniqueness (slugs) or `expect_rev`. The browser can show "pending" only for non-confluent ops, and the MCP can tell agents which ops are safe to batch without `expect_rev`. About +30 lines across the schema emitter and the UI. The risk is misclassification causing a surprising rollback; classify conservatively.
- **C9.** Extend `text::fold` toward NFKC-casefold for fullwidth and compatibility forms. Segment keyword words with UAX #29 word rules, using a character-bigram fallback for Han, Kana and Hangul runs (the bigram fallback is standard search-engine practice, not an Observer card). Add non-English stop lists only as data. Bump `keywords::VERSION` to trigger the backfill. Binary cost of Unicode property tables in wasm is unmeasured (likely tens to low hundreds of KB). On wasm size, the team's notes record the operator's direction to keep improving it "usually with faster, better data layouts, or compression"; whether these tables earn their bytes is the operator's call. A Daciuk-style minimal DFA could store the static lexicon and stop lists compactly. Value depends on how many non-Latin listings the campaign targets.
- **C10.** A Myers diff of CV text versions in the kernel's `history`/compose for the focus lens, roughly 100 lines or a small crate. It is low-risk and low-impact, and useful if agents propose CV edits as diffs.
- **Also-rans (cited above or in the table, not shortlisted):** a typed `Untrusted` text discipline after the IFC survey ([card 881](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/881-language-based-information-flow-security.md)), a cheap partial version of noninterference at kernel and MCP boundaries, which overlaps with the agents researcher's prompt-injection scope. Aho–Corasick for coverage ([card 753](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/753-efficient-string-matching-an-aid-to-bibliographic-search.md)): coverage is one `find` per target over the CV text, but it is cached per application and is not on a measured hot path. Better: tokenize each CV version once and cache by version. Predictive frame budgeting ([card 1162](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1162-adaptive-display-algorithm-for-interactive-frame-rates.md)) becomes moot once C1–C4 land. Per-derive arenas and allocators for wasm ([cards 343](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/343-per-frame-arena-allocation-patterns-in-games.md), [1839](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1839-fast-allocation-and-deallocation-of-memory-based-on-object-lifetimes.md), [011](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/011-mimalloc-free-list-sharding-in-action.md)): measure allocation share first. `rust-code-analysis` LLOC metrics for the LOC warden ([card 963](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/963-rust-code-analysis.md)).

#### Order of work

C1 and C2 first: small, independent, and together they remove every measured cost a non-board write pays today. C3 and C4 then shrink the remaining full passes (rule edits, stage writes). C5 targets the cold path that misses its bound on the worst-case desk. C6 is the UX and correctness upgrade for the rule editor, and the most novel item. C7–C10 are independent and can be taken opportunistically.

### Gaps

- Expected speedups for C3–C5 are estimates; only the baselines were measured. Each needs `bench/kernel.mjs` extended with a picks and rules case (the scratch script above shows how) before and after.
- Not verified: whether Feldera's DBSP crate or differential-dataflow builds for `wasm32-unknown-unknown`, and what binary size it would add. This is moot if the algebra is adopted without the crate, as recommended.
- Wasm size of Unicode word-break and NFKC tables was not measured.
- Heat verdict dependencies beyond the company and vendor groups (department and clone penalties, `kept` seats) were not fully traced; C4's worst case depends on them.
- The 31 ms open-after-write figure assumes the CV lens is in use (rules present). With no rules the cost is 0.469 ms. The operator's actual rule count is unknown.

## Which seemingly relevant cards should hireme not adopt, and why?

### Takeaway

Do not import an engine: no Datalog or IVM runtime, no browser GPU query engine, no embedded SQL engine. hireme needs the delta algebra, not a second rule language. The concurrency, allocator and approximation shelves (lock-free structures, learned indexes, sketches) target problems hireme does not have at 10k rows on one thread. Lockstep-style netcode, CRDTs and vector clocks solve peer-to-peer ordering that a single per-account sequencer already provides.

### Cited Findings

- **IVM and Datalog engines as dependencies.** DDlog compiles Datalog to an incremental Rust engine on Differential Dataflow, but its GitHub repository is an archive. FlowLog is a SPLASH/ISSTA 2026 tool demonstration on DOOP points-to, "not on a game tick". Nemo is a short ICLP 2023 system demo whose own card says to validate incremental and retract semantics before relying on it. DBSP's card says its benchmarks are deferred and "do not invent timings". A 2026 preprint shows that the cheap "first zero delta" fixpoint test is unsound under delta-of-deltas incrementalization, an extra correctness hazard of general recursive engines. — [card 1809](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1809-differential-datalog.md), [card 1593](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1593-flowlog-re-thinking-datalog-for-fast-and-extensible.md), [card 1790](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1790-nemo-first-glimpse-of-a-new-rule-engine.md), [card 1489](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1489-dbsp-automatic-incremental-view-maintenance-for-rich-query-languages.md), [card 1856](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1856-fixing-the-fixpoint-convergence-detection-incremental-recursion.md), [card 823](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/823-differential-dataflow.md), [card 1480](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1480-naiad-a-timely-dataflow-system.md)
- **hireme's governing constraint.** rebuild.md states "One implementation of every rule, run in three places" and lists "A second implementation of any rule in Elixir." under Refused. The kernel's wasm is already 0.9–1.0 MB. — [rebuild.md](../rebuild.md), measurements above
- **Browser and accelerator query engines.** WGLog (WebGPU Datalog, 2026) wins on terascale closures, but small graphs lose to CUDA (0.40× on its smallest input) because WebGPU fixed overhead dominates, and a WebGPU readback costs about 2.5 ms. SRDatalog (GPU worst-case-optimal-join Datalog), FliX (GPU indexing) and TPU query processing target sizes hireme never reaches. hireme's replica reads already run at ≤ 0.4 ms p99. — [card 1094](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1094-terascale-query-processing-in-the-browser-wglog.md), [card 170](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/170-scaling-worst-case-optimal-datalog-to-gpus.md), [card 1093](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1093-flix-flipped-indexing-for-scalable-gpu-queries-and-updates.md), [card 2352](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/2352-the-case-for-tpu-accelerated-analytical-query-processing.md), [bench/kernel.mjs](../../bench/kernel.mjs)
- **Embedded analytic engines.** DataFusion is a Rust Arrow-native engine with replaceable plans and operators, and DuckDB is in-process vectorized OLAP. Both would sit beside the kernel, not replace it. — [card 875](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/875-apache-arrow-datafusion-and-ballista-query-engines.md), [card 874](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/874-duckdb-an-embeddable-analytical-database.md)
- **Worst-case optimal joins.** Their gains come from cyclic queries, via the AGM bound and Free Join's lazy tries. hireme's joins are acyclic foreign-key lookups (application → place → country, application → corporation). — [card 1550](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1550-size-bounds-and-query-plans-for-relational-joins.md), [card 1554](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1554-free-join-unifying-worst-case-optimal-and-traditional-joins.md), [pick.rs:191-210](../../native/kernel/src/pick.rs#L191)
- **Learned indexes, sketches, filters.** These trade exactness for space or speed on large data: learned indexes, HyperLogLog, Bloom/Ribbon. A deterministic kernel at 10k rows answers exactly in under a millisecond. — [card 818](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/818-the-case-for-learned-index-structures.md), [card 842](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/842-hyperloglog-the-analysis-of-a-near-optimal-cardinality-estim.md), [bench/kernel.mjs](../../bench/kernel.mjs)
- **Persistent and purely functional structures for base ⊕ pending.** Fat nodes and path copying, HAMTs, RRB vectors and finger trees keep old versions cheaply. hireme already undoes and reapplies pending writes with cost proportional to the pending ops, and its dense columns serve scans better than tries. — [card 1461](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1461-making-data-structures-persistent.md), [card 1463](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1463-ideal-hash-trees.md), [card 1496](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1496-rrb-vector-a-practical-general-purpose-immutable-sequence.md), [rows.rs header](../../native/kernel/src/rows.rs)
- **Lockstep and input-delay netcode, clocks, CRDTs.** Lockstep sends only inputs and requires bit-exact peers, and frame n cannot advance without input n. hireme is server-authoritative with a total order per account from its sequencer. Lamport and vector clocks order events without a sequencer, which hireme has. The corpus holds no CRDT card. — [card 016](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/016-deterministic-lockstep.md), [card 785](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/785-time-clocks-and-the-ordering-of-events-in-a-distributed-syst.md), [card 786](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/786-timestamps-in-message-passing-systems-that-preserve-the-part.md), [rebuild.md "The sequencer's checks"](../rebuild.md)
- **Float-determinism research.** IEEE floats are deterministic on the same compiler, ISA and FP state, with transcendentals and fused multiply-add as the usual desync sources. hireme already routes float math through `libm` and denies float methods via clippy for cross-target bit-identity. The double-rounding result matters only when emulating narrow formats. — [card 276](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/276-floating-point-determinism.md), [card 2249](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/2249-when-double-rounding-is-correct.md), [kernel Cargo.toml](../../native/kernel/Cargo.toml), [lib.rs header](../../native/kernel/src/lib.rs)
- **The concurrency shelf.** In-scope topic counts include `lockfree` 54, `memory-allocation` 40, `queues` 16 and `work-stealing-schedulers` 12. The kernel runs single-threaded per account (wasm on the page; a per-account NIF resource called by the sequencer with `judge` under 200 µs), and the BEAM owns concurrency. — [Observer MCP endpoint](https://observer.anoptic.io/mcp) (counts computed on the 2,360-card mirror), [rebuild.md "Kernel and NIF"](../rebuild.md)
- **Heavy language technology for a tiny DSL.** Equality saturation, copy-and-patch compilation, Futamura-style partial evaluation (weval) and SMT-backed refinement types solve compiler-scale problems. The mask language is a few dozen atoms per rule set, where batched evaluation (C3) and finite-domain analysis (C6) suffice; Liquid Types need an SMT solver. — [card 1152](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1152-egg-fast-and-extensible-equality-saturation.md), [card 1429](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1429-copy-and-patch-compilation.md), [card 1557](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1557-partial-evaluation-whole-program-compilation.md), [card 905](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/905-liquid-types.md)

- **SIMD byte scanning.** SIMD UTF-8 validation (over 10 GiB/s), vectorized HTML classification and forgiving base64 decoding help at gigabytes. Listings are at most 1 MiB per op, Rust's std already validates UTF-8, and none of these sits on a measured hot path. — [card 1479](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1479-validating-utf-8-in-less-than-one-instruction-per-byte.md), [card 2329](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/2329-scanning-html-at-tens-of-gigabytes-per-second-on-arm-processors.md), [card 2290](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/2290-fast-forgiving-base64-decoding-with-simd-instructions.md), [rebuild.md "Wire v2"](../rebuild.md)

### Inferences

- The common thread: the corpus's engines are built for scale (billions of facts, GPUs, clusters) or for problems hireme removed by design (peer ordering, migrations, multithreading). Adopting them would add a second rule language or megabytes of wasm, contradicting the one-kernel rule and the size pressure, for no measured gain. Each one's algebra or invariant, though, is worth stealing: Z-set deltas from DBSP, conflict-set maintenance from TREAT, acked-baseline deltas from Quake III, and I-confluence from coordination avoidance.
- Typestate and session types (cards 1155, 916/917) also look relevant (pipeline stages, wire protocol) but buy little. hireme's stages are runtime data with legal moves already tabulated in `pipeline.rs`, and the wire is a small fixed frame set.

### Gaps

- The binary-size cost of each rejected engine in wasm was not measured; the rejections rest on architecture (second rule language) and scale mismatch, not on size numbers.
- The corpus has no CRDT, Salsa or incremental-build-system cards, so those alternatives were judged from general knowledge, not from Observer evidence.
