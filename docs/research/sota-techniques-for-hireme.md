# Deterministic checks beat smarter agents for hireme

Hireme gets more from deterministic checks in its one kernel than from smarter agents, and five such checks stand out. First, make CV picks row-local: opening an application right after any application write takes a measured **31.2 ms** at 10,000 applications with eight rules, against **0.086 ms** when cached, a breach of the sub-10 ms rule that a few dozen lines of incremental view maintenance remove. Second, give every agent write one contract: a client idempotency key, an ACK that reports what the kernel derived and what looks wrong, a ledger-checked release report, and read stamps that expose stale plans. Third, bind FIRE HOLD's `open_fire` to the exact CV version, letter and listing it approves, which the code does not do today. Fourth, verify cited quotes and tailored CV and letter claims against stored text before anything reaches an employer. Fifth, replace frequency keywords, found **73% boilerplate** on 900 real listings in an independent test, with named skills and requirement lines. The 2025–2026 literature explains the pattern: wherever server-side machinery and model judgment were compared, the machinery won, as idempotency keys cut duplicate writes from **28% to 4%**, outcome receipts lifted task completion from **10.9% to 28.1%**, deterministic privilege control cut attack success from **39.9% to 1.0%** while detectors fell to adaptive attacks above **90%**, and prompt guardrails still left **half** of tailored CVs fabricated. Hireme's architecture already matches that frontier, so every database, dataflow, CRDT, SMT and rule engine the sweeps surfaced is an anti-candidate, and an independent second-opinion planset converged on eight of the same techniques, including the idempotency key and a harness-first build order. The limits are real: most agent evidence is single-group preprints from August to October 2026, hireme's latency figures come from Bun with synthetic rules, and no one has yet counted hireme's own overrides, outcomes or duplicated retries.

## The Observer supplied mechanisms, not hiring research

Six research lanes ran on 20 October 2026. Two swept the Broadside Observer corpus pool by pool: one over the agents, game-AI and realtime pools (426 in-scope cards, 773 queries), the other over systems, languages, engines and foundations (1,252 in-scope titles once 372 AI-generated mathematics cards were set aside, 524 query specs). Four searched the wider literature on job matching and preference learning, CV and document generation, MCP agent security, and incremental sync and encoding. The hosted MCP turned away scripted sweeps, answering HTTP 403 to Python's urllib while curl succeeded and a Cloudflare 1015 limit to the second-opinion researcher, so both Observer lanes re-implemented `query_seeds`' tokenized substring search over a mirror at the same commit, calibrated it against live calls (exact on three of four queries, 205 against 206 on the fourth), and confirmed **131 cards** through `get_seed`, each byte-identical to the mirror ([agents sweep](sota-techniques-for-hireme-observer-agents-ai.md); [systems sweep](sota-techniques-for-hireme-observer-systems-languages.md); [Observer MCP](https://observer.anoptic.io/mcp)).

The corpus answered some questions richly and others not at all. Queries for hiring, recruiting, career, recommender, explanation, calibration, CRDT and Elixir returned **zero cards**, and the second opinion counted two cards mentioning BM25 and none on learning-to-rank or inverted indexes ([agents sweep](sota-techniques-for-hireme-observer-agents-ai.md); [planset](sonnet-researcher-planset.md)). Where hireme hurts most, though, the shelf is deep: incremental computation from Rete (1982) to a 2026 OOPSLA commit-window contract and an October 2026 dirty-set measurement; 239 agent cards, 136 of them from 2026, dominated by harness, failure-localization and provenance studies; and netcode cards that mostly confirm hireme's prediction-and-rollback replica. The hireme-specific results on person–job fit, CV fabrication, LLM screening and MCP security therefore come from the outside literature, and the Observer contributes mechanisms.

Two cautions govern everything below. Most agent cards are arXiv preprints one to ten weeks old, often from a single group on a synthetic benchmark (ToolMaze, scripted approval trials, synthetic resumes), so their mechanisms and failure taxonomies deserve more weight than their magnitudes. And hireme's own latency figures come from a Bun 1.4.2 (JavaScriptCore) build at 10,000 applications with eight synthetic rules, not from Chromium on the operator's node. The independent planset used to cross-check this report read about 25 papers past the abstract and ran three experiments on 900 public Greenhouse listings, and it kept its independence by reading only these notes' headings and one idempotency passage after finishing its own plans ([planset](sonnet-researcher-planset.md)).

### 1. Make CV picks row-local and invalidation write-scoped

Opening an application is held to "every UX interaction must be as fast or faster than before (sub-10 ms, measured)", and it is the interaction the sweeps caught breaking that rule. With eight CV rules over 10,000 applications, opening an application right after any application write takes **31.2 ms** (33.8 ms on the worst-case all-distinct desk), against **0.086 ms** when picks are cached and 0.469 ms with no rules; a `notes`-only edit costs the full 31.2 ms, while a `contacts` write costs 0.071 ms ([systems sweep](sota-techniques-for-hireme-observer-systems-languages.md)). The cause sits in `Kernel::moved`: any write to `applications`, `places`, `corporations`, `cv_rules`, `cv_masks`, `cv_roots`, `me` or `languages` sets the pick cache to `None`, and the next `application(id)` rebuilds `Picks::of` over the whole desk, evaluating every enabled rule for every application because each pick also lists every other matching arm ([abi.rs](../../native/kernel/src/abi.rs); [pick.rs](../../native/kernel/src/pick.rs)). The same sweep found a `contacts` write, a table the board never reads, still costing a 3.93 ms board re-derive, and the all-distinct desk's first derive after BOOT at **34.05 ms against its 30 ms bound**.

Incremental view maintenance explains why the fix is small. In DBSP, a VLDB 2023 best paper, linear operators such as map and filter are their own incremental versions, so a change costs work proportional to the change ([DBSP](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol16/p1601-budiu.pdf); [card 1489](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1489-dbsp-automatic-incremental-view-maintenance-for-rich-query-languages.md)). A pick is a row-local map over one application joined to its corporation, its place and four small tables, so its delta is row-local too. The counting algorithm shows that non-recursive views need none of DRed's machinery ([card 1841](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1841-maintaining-views-incrementally.md)), TREAT keeps each rule's match set across cycles instead of recomputing it ([card 1004](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1004-treat-a-better-match-algorithm-for-ai-production-systems.md)), Adapton refreshes only what an observer demands ([card 1466](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1466-adapton-composable-demand-driven-incremental-computation.md)), and Salsa's durability levels let rarely changing inputs such as rules and `me` form an epoch that frequent writes never invalidate ([Salsa](https://salsa-rs.github.io/salsa/reference/algorithm.html)). A 2026 OOPSLA contract for reactive entity-component runtimes supplies the legality argument for coalescing one net-effect delta per cell per commit window, which here is a sequencer turn ([card 1853](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1853-commit-window-observation-contracts-for-reactive-ecs.md)), and an October 2026 measurement found plain delta kernels **2.1–8.2× faster** than the best full recompute while a cached schedule lost in 779 of 837 workloads, with the guideline to always compare against full recompute ([card 2328](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/2328-how-the-dirty-set-moves-schedule-reuse-in-incremental-sparse-matrix-multiplication.md)).

The plan has three steps. First, `moved()` stops dropping all picks on application writes and re-picks only the written ids with the existing per-application `pick::pick`; a write to a place or corporation re-picks the applications that reference it, by reverse index or wholesale since such writes are rare; the full rebuild stays only for the rule epoch (`cv_rules`, `cv_masks`, `cv_roots`, `me`, `languages`); and `application(id)` picks its one application on demand when no `Picks` exist instead of building the whole desk. Second, each derived cache gets a constant table read-set, so writes to `contacts`, `messages`, `events` or `citations` stop marking the board stale. Third, `Moved` carries a per-table column bitset and each derivation declares the columns it reads; `mask::Expr::fields()` already computes a rule's fields, so a `notes` or `letter_raw` edit then costs nothing derived ([systems sweep](sota-techniques-for-hireme-observer-systems-languages.md)). Only after that, and only if a Chromium profile shows derive dominating a stage write, comes the group-local board: heat's fixed-point group totals, already exact in any order, maintained as Z-set deltas, verdicts recomputed for the touched company and vendor groups, and an ordered index replacing the full sort ([systems-sync note](sota-techniques-for-hireme-systems-incremental-sync.md)).

The estimated cost is +15/−10 to +40/−15 lines for the picks and +40/−10 for read-set filtering, with no change in binary size, and the deletions are the wholesale invalidation and build-all-on-open paths. The risk is a missed read dependency that leaves a stale pick or card. The gate is a pair of seeded property tests (incremental picks equal `Picks::of`; an incremental board equals a fresh derive) beside the existing "base ⊕ deltas = fresh read" law, plus a debug assertion against a full derive. The expected result, open-after-write near the 0.1–0.5 ms floor, is an estimate to re-measure in Chromium on the operator's node with a picks case added to `bench/kernel.mjs`. Neither the `dbsp` crate (tokio, nix, C zstd, built only for x86_64) nor Salsa (a September 2026 use-after-free fix, a wasm32 `catch_unwind` history) belongs in the kernel: five statically known derivations do not need a generic dependency graph ([docs.rs dbsp](https://docs.rs/crate/dbsp/latest); [Fedora advisory](https://linuxsecurity.com/advisories/fedora/rust-salsa-fedora-44-2026-407b957a3b)).

Two SOTA lanes found this rebuild independently and both ranked it first. The planset missed it; its proposal to turn `Board::derive` into a dirty set "only after measuring" is borne out by the measurements, which put the board inside its bound and the picks outside.

### 2. Give every agent write one contract: once, loud, accounted, current

Four weaknesses in how agents write are verified in the code. A retried MCP call is a new op: `put` and every command mint `op_id` from a per-session counter and the tool schemas take no client key, so the ledger rule that "an ACKed op id resent, whatever it now carries, answers its first ACK and writes nothing" never applies to an MCP retry, and an append-only `note` or an insert into a table without a natural key can land twice ([mcp.ex](../../lib/hireme_web/mcp.ex); [SECURITY.md](../../SECURITY.md); [planset](sonnet-researcher-planset.md)). The MCP 2026-07-28 revision makes retries routine, since a broken stream loses the in-flight request and clients "MUST re-issue it as a new request with a new request ID" ([MCP changelog](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/changelog)), and hireme's own 80-second deadline already tells agents a pending write "may still land, so find or get before sending it again". Each ACK is one line, `ok: <label> -> id N, rev R`, carrying nothing the kernel derived, so checking a write costs another turn; releases check nothing the agent claims; the audit row for an op batch records counts but no refusal codes; and nothing records what a draft was derived from ([agents sweep](sota-techniques-for-hireme-observer-agents-ai.md)).

Each weakness has a measured remedy in 2026 work. Limbo's 25,930 episodes showed that when a write is in flight or delivered twice, the tool contract explains 81% of outcome variance, frontier models duplicate in **56% and 74%** of episodes, and an idempotency key on every write cut duplicates from **28% to 4%**, because agents use keys when they exist ([Limbo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29095)). Agents notice **91.3%** of explicit errors but only **58.8%** of plausible wrong values, and a prompt asking them to check did not help ([card 2244](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/2244-loud-failures-quiet-failures-fault-detection-and-recovery-in-tool-using.md); [arXiv 2610.10062](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10062)), while nonbinding receipts naming the violated property and a recovery tool lifted ToolMaze completion from **10.9% to 28.1%** ([card 1144](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1144-outcome-monitors-recovery-affordances-for-silent-tool-failure.md); [arXiv 2608.19303](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19303)). Receipts must describe a failure rather than echo the failed call, since echoing it raised small models' odds of repeating it from 0.06 to 0.54 ([card 1368](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1368-feedback-that-backfires-why-small-language-model-agents-repe.md)). Turn count explains **84%** of agent cost against 4% for observation size ([card 2287](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/2287-turncost-turns-not-observations-cache-aware-loop-engineering-for-coding.md)), so an ACK that carries derived state saves a turn per write. Readers of agent reports alone recover **52.0%** of task state against **92.7%** with full context ([card 2313](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/2313-what-coding-agents-leave-unsaid-measuring-task-state-gaps-between.md)), and evidence-carrying termination produced 0 unsafe completions in 288 against 252 for a termination critic ([card 1367](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1367-when-may-an-agent-stop-evidence-carrying-termination-for-too.md)). PlanFence's handoff result shows what session tracking misses: all 30 inherited conflicts, caught only once the inherited versions were stored ([PlanFence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03340)).

The contract has four parts, built in this order. The first is exactly-once delivery: an optional client key `op` on `put` and every command, mapped as `op_id = hash(key_id, op)` into the existing ledger and answered with `replayed: true`. Its tool description tells agents to pin one key per intent and reuse it on retry, as Limbo's discussion argues, and there is no server-derived default, because two identical notes can be intentional. The second is a loud ACK: one line per acknowledged row stating what the kernel derived (score suggestion and band, heat class with `cooldown_days`, pick, coverage), plus conservative `warning[...]` receipts from deterministic checks that each name a recovery tool such as `text`, `history`, `get` or `cv`. Candidate receipts are a listing that yields few keywords or matches login-wall or captcha phrases, a metric more than ten times or under a tenth of its latest cited value, an `as_of` years old, an uploaded CV with empty text, and a new corporation whose normalized name matches an existing one. The same derived values become filterable columns in `find` (band, heat class, coverage, stale, freshness), as the planset proposes, so agents stop polling. The third is an accounted release: `release` returns a report built from the ledger, audit and citations (rows per table, stage moves, citations by host, refusals by code), flags any claim in the agent's own report that names a row the ledger does not back, and tallies refusal codes per key across sessions, which separates interface failures (`type`, `column`) from planning failures (`heat`, `stage`) and research hygiene (`source_missing`, `stale`). The fourth is currency: reads return `(table, id, rev)` stamps, the MCP session keeps what it delivered, writes may carry `deps`, a moved dependency is refused `stale_plan` with what changed so that one repair turn suffices, and agent prose stores a `basis`, so a superseded cell flags the draft stale in truth-maintenance style, flagging and never deleting ([card 2235](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/2235-a-truth-maintenance-system.md)).

The key is the cheapest change in either planset. The ACK digest adds an estimated +60–120 lines and must stay conservative, since agents already raise false alarms on 26.8% of fault-free trials and mined detection falls to 46% outside its vocabulary. The currency part carries a wire cost, because `EXPECT_REV` is one fixed `u32` in the op header and `deps` needs a variable field, which the planset expects to offset by folding `EXPECT_REV` and the prose `rev` refusal into one mechanism. Full-text reading adds two lessons: server-observed reads are only a floor, since S-Bus could see 26.1% of single-step references over HTTP, and the dependency frontier must come from what the judge reads, never from the agent's declaration alone, since a too-narrow declared scope let 66 of 200 invariant violations commit in Atomix ([S-Bus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17076); [Atomix](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14849)). Because per-application leases already block same-row conflicts, `deps` waits until the harness shows cross-row stale writes happen. The gates are that a retried `note` writes one event, a stale dependency never lands, dependency checks stay in microseconds at 10,000 rows, and a replayed session with an injected timeout and duplicate delivery grades clean against `op_ledger` and `events`. The delivered-read log also enables a later flow check in the style of FIDES and FLOWSEAL, refusing a write into a URL column or outreach draft that embeds private values the session was served, such as citizenships, permits or a salary floor, which is hireme's share of the GitHub MCP "toxic agent flow" ([FIDES](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23643v2); [FLOWSEAL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14003); [Invariant Labs](https://invariantlabs.ai/blog/mcp-github-vulnerability)).

This is the most corroborated candidate. The MCP-security lane, reasoning from the spec's re-issue rule, and the planset, reasoning from Limbo, reached the idempotency key independently; by its own account the planset saw the other note's idempotency passage only after finishing its plans. The Observer sweep and the planset both arrived at a ledger-checked release report citing the same termination paper, and both justify fewer turns with TurnCost. The currency part is not independent, because the Observer sweep took it from the planset's first version, and on what PlanFence proves, the planset's full-text reading governs.

### 3. Bind FIRE HOLD's approval to the exact CV, letter and listing

The operator's rule reads: "FIRE HOLD stands: nothing submits an application; naming open fire is a human act (`open_fire` is a `human_only` command)." ([rebuild.md](../rebuild.md)). The kernel enforces who names open fire but not what is approved. `command::OPEN_FIRE` only moves `fire_ready` to `open_fire`; the CV version freezes only on the move to `submitted`, as whatever the pick is then ("Going out freezes the CV version that went: the pick's."); and `pick_cv` and `unpin_cv` carry no stage guard ([judge.rs](../../native/kernel/src/judge.rs)). Between the operator's approval and the submission, an agent can re-pick or unpin the CV, add a new head version to the picked root, or rewrite `letter_raw`. The application stays approved while what it would send changes, and the CV recorded as sent need not be the one the operator saw.

"Approve What, Exactly?" (15 October 2026) names this class of failure. An approval is a real second factor only if the agent cannot produce it and it names the exact effect that will run; twelve approval-path attacks broke AP2, a Microsoft action-bound approval design and MCP elicitation; and an audit found "post-approval argument changes in four of eight runtimes with measurable binding". Rendering the approval from a canonical effect descriptor and signing its digest cut forged-narration success from **0.54 to 0.03** over 27,108 scripted trials, with code, data and 53 Tamarin lemmas released ([arXiv 2610.21063](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21063); [card 2314](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/2314-approve-what-exactly-effect-binding-and-trusted-rendering-for-agent.md)). A companion paper finds that most deployed-agent exploits approve a name that later resolves to a different object, so the binding must hold the object and be re-checked at use ([card 2284](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/2284-names-are-not-objects-object-bound-confinement-for-compromised-ai.md)). An observational study of agentic coding with FIRE HOLD's shape, in which a human approves a contract and deterministic hooks enforce it, finds that stopping is the weak point ([card 2241](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/2241-humanize-judgement-engineering-for-agentic-coding.md)).

The plan moves the existing freeze block from the `submitted` path to `open_fire`, so the approval itself fixes `cv_version_id` from the pick, and writes a canonical descriptor into the `open_fire` event body: the application's number, the CV version id and blob SHA, a hash of `letter_raw`, and the listing's `canonical_url` with a hash of `listing_raw`. While an application sits at `open_fire`, agent ops that touch a bound input (`pick_cv`, `unpin_cv`, `letter_raw`, `listing_raw`, or a new version on the bound root) meet the existing `fire_hold` refusal, whose help already says "No agent opens fire or submits. Leave the application at fire_ready.", with a new reason, "bound since open_fire" ([diag.rs](../../native/mcp/src/diag.rs)). An operator edit to a bound input demotes the application to `fire_ready` with an event, voiding the approval until a one-click re-approval. The focus lens renders the descriptor beside the fire control, which is the paper's trusted rendering, and the move to `submitted` records the bound version after checking that the descriptor still matches.

The estimated cost is 30–60 changed kernel lines, one diagnostic reason and one test, with no wire or schema change while the hashes ride the event body, and part of it replaces the freeze-at-submit code ([agents sweep](sota-techniques-for-hireme-observer-agents-ai.md)). The friction is a re-approval after a late edit, and agents that polish letters after approval meet a clear refusal instead of silently changing what goes out. A phone passkey or second device adds nothing, since the same paper found a second device "added nothing measurable over a trusted on-host confirmation". A second phase applies the binding where the planset aimed it: the passkey step-up for widening a key is a generic five-minute freshness window with a random challenge, and could instead sign the mask change it authorizes ([planset](sonnet-researcher-planset.md)). A third phase, only if needed, routes rare high-risk agent actions through MCP 2026-07-28 URL-mode elicitation to hireme's own approval page, kept rare because Claude Code users approve 93% of permission prompts ([MCP elicitation](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/client/elicitation); [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/claude-code-auto-mode)).

Both plansets read this paper and aimed it at different targets. The planset applied it to step-up and left it out of its top five as an "Oct 2026 preprint, no human data". That critique fits step-up, where the operator's passkey already gates the act, but not `open_fire`, where the gap is a logical one verified in the code and the fix borrows the paper's design pattern rather than its magnitudes. The Observer sweep's application is the stronger case.

### 4. Check every quote and claim against stored text

Hireme already avoids most fabrication by composing each CV from the operator's own items under bounded overlays, and cv.rs states the guard: "Every overlay belongs to its corporation's lineage, so every application to one company tells the same story." ([cv.rs](../../native/kernel/src/cv.rs)). Two gaps remain. Citation rows hold `table`, `row_id`, `column`, `source_id`, `value`, `as_of`, `actor`, `key_id` and `op_id` but no quote or span, so a cited headcount or pay figure is only as good as the agent's transcription ([hireme.schema](../../schema/hireme.schema)). And the free-text surfaces, `tailor` overlays that rewrite an item's title or body and `letter_raw`, pass no support check ([CV note](sota-techniques-for-hireme-cv-documents-generation.md)).

The evidence is strong on both. In a 180-run study of hiring pipelines, **96.7%** of fully automated outputs carried at least one unsupported claim (**90.0%** with a newer model); prompt guardrails cut finding density by 86% yet half the outputs still fabricated; a human checkpoint removed only **54.5%** of "subtle qualifier drops and plausible new claims"; and requirements planted in the job description leaked into 47% of baseline outputs ([Takano](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26171)). That is a single-author preprint on synthetic resumes, but it is the only controlled study found. Citation is the known weak spot of research agents: the best ALCE models lack complete support **50%** of the time, deep-research systems cite correctly **40–80%** of the time, attribution judges reach about 80% macro-F1, and factuality metrics penalize paraphrase ([ALCE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14627); [DeepTRACE](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.04499v1); [AttributionBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15089); [Godbole & Jia](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14883)). Extractive grounding works: Anthropic's Citations API extracts `cited_text` so citations "are guaranteed to contain valid pointers", and LangExtract grounds each extraction to a character span or returns none ([Anthropic Citations](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/citations); [LangExtract](https://github.com/google/langextract)). Polish no longer pays either: one standard deviation of customization was worth a $25.67 lower bid on Freelancer.com before LLMs and $10.82 after, and on another platform the alignment–callback correlation fell 51% after an AI letter tool launched, while time spent editing drafts tracked hiring success ([Galdin & Silbert](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08785); [Cui et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25054)).

The plan adds two deterministic gates that run identically in WebAssembly and the NIF. The first anchors provenance in quotes. `put` gains a bounded `quote`, and after the existing `source_missing` check the provenance check requires the normalized quote to occur in the source's stored text (`listing_raw` or a `sources.doc` snapshot) and, for a metric, requires the cell's value to be parseable from the quote with unit scaling for k, m, bn, percentages and currency. `citations` gains an offset and length, refusals `quote_missing` and `quote_mismatch` carry diagnostic help, and `history` and the world lens highlight the quote. The kernel trusts the quote, never an agent's offsets, and a field with no verbatim basis ("remote" inferred from "work from anywhere") carries a short quote and an `inferred` flag. The second gates claims. Each `tailor` or `add` overlay is compared with its source item, and with the rest of the operator's corpus as weaker support, on four token classes: numbers and dates; capitalized names, organizations and tools; listing keywords absent from the source; and scope words where hedges ("helped", "contributed to") become ownership ("led", "owned", "architected"). The gate warns rather than refuses, and the operator accepts an unsupported token with a justification in the existing `cv_overlays.reason` column. Letters follow the same rule: factual sentences cite an item with an inline reference that the kernel resolves and the renderer strips. Entailment checks with a MiniCheck-class model stay agent-side and advisory ([MiniCheck](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10774)).

The estimates are +100–200 lines for quotes and +150–250 for the claim gate, both net positive, so each needs a named payback; the MCP-security lane suggests retiring the prose-only provenance branches that the quote check supersedes. Normalization is the main technical risk: the keyword stripper can be reused, but `text.rs` folding covers Latin scripts only. An agent can fabricate a snapshot together with its quote; a stored digest, corroboration across keys and snapshots, and an operator "verified" mark contain that, and the node itself must never fetch agent URLs. Legitimate rewording draws false alarms only within the four token classes, and inflation that adds no new tokens passes, which is the advisory entailment pass's job. A natural follow-on checks CV files at upload for parse health and hidden text, because employer detectors now compare rendered pages with extracted text and about 1% of roughly 200,000 real resumes carried hidden prompt injections ([arXiv 2605.28999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28999)).

Two SOTA lanes reached quote-verified provenance independently, the CV lane from extraction grounding and the MCP-security lane from citation research, and each ranked it first in its scorecard. The planset has no counterpart (its completion receipts are the nearest relative), so this candidate rests on the external literature and those two notes, with nothing to adjudicate.

### 5. Replace frequency keywords with named skills and requirement lines

`keywords.rs` keeps a listing's 12 most frequent words (ties by bytes; `TOP = 12`, `VERSION = 5`) after discarding every token shorter than three characters unless it contains `#`, and coverage counts whole-term hits in the CV's folded text ([keywords.rs](../../native/kernel/src/keywords.rs)). The planset re-implemented that extractor over 900 public engineering listings from 15 Greenhouse boards. **73%** of the words it chose appear in 30% or more of all listings; tech-term precision@12 is 0.04 and recall@12 **0.08** against a proxy list of about 150 terms; tf-idf strips the boilerplate (0.04) without recovering skills (recall still 0.08), and BM25 does worse; and "ai" (847 listings), "go" (357), "ml" (187), "ci" (173), "c" and "r" can never become targets. A listing names a mean of 6.7 distinct tech terms, mostly once or twice, which no frequency or IDF ranking selects, but listings carry structure: a median of 21 bullets, a requirements-style heading in 732 of 900 and a nice-to-have marker in 759, with requirement bullets holding 0.61 tech terms each against 0.34 for responsibilities. The results held across four random samples; the caveats are the researcher's own term list and a large-company, English, Greenhouse-only sample ([planset](sonnet-researcher-planset.md)).

The literature agrees on the direction. In-context LLMs locate skills but miss span boundaries, with GPT-4's strict span F1 of 36.8–37.7 trailing 64.4 for a supervised tagger while relaxed F1 reaches 58.0, and an LLM reranking taxonomy candidates added over 22 points of RP@10 ([Nguyen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03832); [Clavié & Soulié](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.03539)). TalentCLEF 2026's winner reached 0.7140 MAP against a 0.3828 baseline, and the organizers concluded that "the strongest systems... used hybrid, modular pipelines rather than single-model retrieval" ([TalentCLEF 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31692v1)). Literal matching still matters, since 88% of employers in the Hidden Workers survey agreed that exact-criteria filters screen out qualified candidates ([TechRadar on Hidden Workers](https://www.techradar.com/news/automated-hiring-software-is-rejecting-millions-of-suitable-job-candidates)).

The design stays inside the operator's rule on listings: "We do not care about parsing job listings out into more database columns(at all!). Just throw the entire RAW unedited text a job listing came with into this text row, for any given listing." ([rebuild.md](../rebuild.md)). It changes what the kernel derives from the raw text into the existing versioned `keywords` column and adds no parsed listing columns. The first layer needs no model: a skill lexicon with aliases (k8s and kubernetes, postgres and postgresql, golang and go) and a short-token allowlist, seeded from the operator's own CV items and from ESCO v1.2's alternative labels (reuse licence still to verify), compiled into the existing `lexicon.rs` trie ([ESCO v1.2](https://esco.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2024-05/V1.2%20Launch%20-%20ESCO%20SEC%20combined%20-%20final%20clean.pdf)). Requirement lines are the bullets under requirements-style headings, split into must-have and nice-to-have by marker. Targets become about seven named skills plus the requirement lines, and bumping `keywords::VERSION` triggers the server's existing re-extraction backfill. The application document then reports exact coverage, which literal filters see, beside alias coverage, which people and semantic screeners see, and sorts gaps into "coverable" (an item left out covers it) and "uncovered" (no record covers it, so the letter addresses it honestly and nothing is fabricated). From there `compose` can suggest overlays that maximize saturated coverage of the requirement lines under the page's line budget; greedy selection carries the best-possible (1 − 1/e) guarantee and tens of items permit an exact search ([Khuller, Moss & Naor](https://sites.umiacs.umd.edu/node/15228)), always inside the lineage rule quoted above. Static embeddings, an int8 Model2Vec-class table, enter only if the lexicon's recall falls short on the eval set, and below a confidence margin the kernel answers "uncertain" and a leased agent judges, its verdict stored as cited data.

The eval comes first: 200 operator-judged pairs of listing and CV item plus the 900-listing set, scored on requirement recall and nDCG@10, with derivation under 5 ms per application at 10,000 ([planset](sonnet-researcher-planset.md)). The change is net positive in lines, because lexicon data counts as tracked text, so it needs the operator's waiver or a named payback even though it retires the frequency heuristics and stop list. Non-English listings need per-language lexicons, and nothing measured covers them. The scope is also narrower than it looks: coverage is read only by the focus lens and the MCP application document ([compose.rs](../../native/kernel/src/compose.rs); [focus.ts](../../assets/js/lenses/focus.ts)), and `score.rs`, `heat.rs` and `board.rs` do not read keywords, so the gain is in CV tailoring and fit judgment, and whether fit should ever move the Life-EV score is the operator's decision.

The matching lane (requirement extraction with verbatim span checks, concept normalization) and the CV lane (alias coverage, budgeted selection with gap triage) reached the planset's diagnosis independently, and they differ on the first step. The matching lane leads with agent-extracted requirement rows, an ESCO concept table and in-kernel static embeddings; the planset leads with a kernel lexicon and listing structure. The planset's case is stronger on three counts: it measured the gap; Model2Vec's retrieval figures are English-only while its multilingual model weighs roughly 0.4–0.5 GB; and new requirement tables are what the operator's listing rule declines. The matching lane's verbatim-span check survives as the guard on any requirement or alias an agent proposes.

### Build the harness first, then three small fixes in parallel

All three research lines reached the same order independently: build a replayable agent harness before any agent-facing change, because one run proves nothing when fault-free reruns end in the same state only 63.3% of the time ([card 2244](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/2244-loud-failures-quiet-failures-fault-detection-and-recovery-in-tool-using.md)) and description rewrites regressed 16.67% of cases in one MCP study ([MCP tool descriptions](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2602.14878)). The harness lives in `bench/` beside the perf harness: a seeded bench account (a `me` row, two CVs, about 30 applications, listings and mail carrying injection canaries), 20 to 40 user tasks each graded by diffing final SQLite state against a goal state, five trials per task on two or three clients, and reports of pass^k, tokens, tool calls and refusals by code, plus a Limbo-style replay that injects a timeout and a duplicate delivery ([MCP-security note](sota-techniques-for-hireme-agents-mcp-security.md); [planset](sonnet-researcher-planset.md)). It records reads and turns inside the test process, which keeps it within the operator's instruction, as the MCP-security note quotes it: "Do not write routine persistent diagnostic logs; retain diagnostics only when necessary for errors, hard crashes, or explicitly requested troubleshooting". Three fixes then touch disjoint files and can land in parallel without the harness: row-local picks (`abi.rs`, `pick.rs`), the `open_fire` binding (`judge.rs`, `diag.rs`) and the idempotency key (`native/mcp`, `mcp.ex`). The loud ACK and release report follow, measured against the harness; then quote and claim verification; then skill-aware fit once its eval set exists; and last the dependency stamps, only if the harness shows cross-row stale writes.

The eight genuine convergences are not equally strong. The harness-first order and the idempotency key rest on the best evidence, a mandatory spec rule and a large measured study reached by separate routes. The skill-aware fit convergence is strong on the diagnosis because the planset measured what the matching lane inferred from reading the code. The Bayesian residual convergence is weak in substance: both sides admit it rests on general methods with no single-seeker evidence ([matching note](sota-techniques-for-hireme-matching-ranking-learning.md)), and the planset's own first step, "count the overrides that exist", is the right gate.

Where the two disagree, the evidence settles each case. On PlanFence, the Observer sweep read the abstract (30 of 30 correct against a freshness-only executor that acted on the stale plan every time, at 143–237 ms of stall per action), while the planset read the paper and found that centralized lineage also scores 30 of 30. PlanFence's edge is stall time in distributed settings, which does not apply to hireme's local sequencer, and the result that transfers is the handoff table, where session read tracking missed all 30 inherited conflicts until inherited versions were stored ([PlanFence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03340); [planset](sonnet-researcher-planset.md)). The planset's reading is stronger, and it moves candidate 2 toward storing a `basis` with each draft rather than trusting session tracking. On priority, the Observer sweep ranked read-set validation seventh because per-application leases already block same-row conflicts, while the planset built its write contract first. Each is right about a different part: the idempotency key is cheap and measured, while `deps` costs a wire change and guards a cross-row case not yet observed in hireme.

On embeddings, the matching lane scored in-kernel static embeddings fused with BM25 as high-impact, while the planset deferred them behind a lexicon. The planset's evidence is stronger: Model2Vec's retrieval figures are English, its multilingual model has 128 million parameters and cannot ride a cold load at roughly 0.4–0.5 GB, and an agent study found grep generally beat vector retrieval, though not uniformly ([potion-multilingual-128m](https://huggingface.co/minishlab/potion-multilingual-128m); [Is Grep All You Need?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15184)). On structured listing extraction, the MCP-security lane's JSON-LD `JobPosting` parse into typed listing columns and the matching lane's agent-extracted requirement rows both run against the operator's rule quoted in candidate 5, while the planset's kernel lexicon does not, and the operator's decision governs.

On outcomes, the matching lane ranked a censoring-aware reply and ghosting model first, while the planset and the Observer sweep held that one operator's outcomes are too few. Both positions hold in part. Treating "no reply yet" as censored rather than negative is correct at any sample size and yields a pooled ghost probability that rises with silence ([Chapelle](https://unpaywall.org/10.1145%2F2623330.2623634); [Maller et al.](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/statistics-surveys/volume-18/issue-none/Mixture-cure-model-methodology-in-survival-analysis--Some-recent/10.1214/24-SS147.full)), but segment rates by ATS vendor, company size or channel need counts nobody has taken. Thompson sampling for daily batches, which the matching lane itself ranked fourteenth of fifteen, waits for a posterior worth sampling. On delta resume, the systems sweep offered an in-memory ring of recent PATCH bodies and the planset a change log persisted in the sequencer's own transaction; the planset is right, because a deploy restarts the BEAM and the existing `op_ledger` misses cascades and server-side writes such as keyword backfills. Finally, the systems-sync note says `Board::derive` rebuilds the heat governor index on every derive, but the code at HEAD keeps the index across derives for the same day and configuration and recomputes only each card's verdict and the sort, as the planset reads it ([board.rs](../../native/kernel/src/board.rs)).

## Take the algebra, not the engines

Hireme's core already sits where the literature ends up. In centralized systems, CRDTs and operational transforms are "merely optimizations over server reconciliation", the undo-apply-redo model hireme's replica runs ([Weidner](https://mattweidner.com/2024/06/04/server-architectures.html)). Heat caps, leases and FIRE HOLD fail the invariant-confluence test, so they need the sequencer ([card 845](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/845-coordination-avoidance-in-database-systems.md)). Fixed-schema stores survive a writer-model swap with an accuracy change of **+0.0004 ± 0.0020** while model-written notes do not ([card 1642](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1642-does-your-agents-memory-survive-a-model-upgrade-a-controlled-study.md)), and masks and leases checked outside the model's context are the typed capabilities a 2026 harness study recommends ([card 1767](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1767-authority-is-not-a-string-a-capability-scoped-harness-for-prompt.md)). What the corpus adds is algebra: Z-set deltas from DBSP, match sets from TREAT, acknowledged-baseline deltas from Quake III, invariant confluence from coordination avoidance. Its engines are anti-candidates, because each would be a second rule implementation against "One implementation of every rule, run in three places." ([rebuild.md](../rebuild.md)) or megabytes of WebAssembly. The `dbsp` crate pulls in tokio, nix and C zstd and builds only for x86_64; Salsa needed a September 2026 use-after-free fix; Z3 for the browser is a **34.6 MB** WebAssembly file, about 38 times the kernel; Rete and Phreak buy nothing for rules that each match one application; memory64 runs 10% to over 100% slower; and relaxed SIMD returns implementation-defined results that would let the browser's prediction disagree with the NIF's verdict ([docs.rs dbsp](https://docs.rs/crate/dbsp/latest); [Fedora advisory](https://linuxsecurity.com/advisories/fedora/rust-salsa-fedora-44-2026-407b957a3b); [z3-solver package](https://data.jsdelivr.com/v1/packages/npm/z3-solver@5.3.1); [SpiderMonkey](https://spidermonkey.dev/blog/2025/01/15/is-memory64-actually-worth-using.html); [caniuse](https://caniuse.com/wf-wasm-simd-relaxed)).

On the agent side, the anti-candidates are the defenses and loops the measurements broke. Detectors and guardrail classifiers fell to adaptive attacks at success rates from 71% upward, most above 90% and one in every case, so at most they are telemetry ([arXiv 2510.09023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09023)). LLM or NLI verdicts gating writes inherit the 80% F1 of attribution judges, and cross-model votes over shared evidence still approve unsafe proposals, because verifier diversity is not evidence diversity ([card 1803](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1803-engineering-reliable-commit-gates-for-agentic-ai-cost-aware.md)). Confirming every write trains approval fatigue, and having the node fetch agent-supplied URLs to check sources is the SSRF exposure the MCP security guidance warns against ([MCP security](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/basic/security_best_practices)). Designing for agent fan-out moved success between −11.9 and +2.3 points while tokens rose 1.41–3.31× ([card 2243](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/2243-when-sub-agents-work-in-parallel-the-promises-and-pitfalls-of-dynamic.md)). Self-evolving skill libraries authored unsafe artifacts in all 21 evolved configurations, and keep-if-better selection inflated gains by 13–20 points with 16 selection items ([card 1015](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/1015-practice-makes-unsafe-skill-misevolution-in-self-improving-llm-agents.md); [card 2242](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/2242-the-winner-s-curse-in-llm-self-improvement-loops-selection-noise-lock.md)), which also rules out reply-rate bandits that would auto-optimize CV choice on one seeker's few hundred outcomes.

On the product side, three temptations fail on the evidence and on the operator's rules. Auto-apply and mass tailoring violate FIRE HOLD and chase a signal LLMs have already devalued. Keyword stuffing and hidden text work mainly on the least valid screeners and are now detected in production by comparing rendered pages with extracted text ([arXiv 2507.11548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11548); [arXiv 2605.28999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28999)). Platform person–job-fit models, up to LinkedIn's 150-billion-parameter 360Brew, need two-sided interaction data one operator cannot produce, and transformer encoders in browser WebAssembly take 451–507 ms per 512-token input ([360Brew](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2501.16450); [browser embedding benchmark](https://huggingface.co/spaces/Xenova/webgpu-embedding-benchmark/discussions/1)).

## Conclusion

The frontier has converged on hireme's shape. The most consequential 2026 agent results, from effect-bound approvals and outcome receipts to policy compiled into deterministic gates and humans approving contracts that hooks enforce, all place reliability in machinery outside the model, which is what a single deterministic kernel judging every write already is ([card 2257](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/2257-nomos-compiling-written-policies-into-statically-verified-tool-call-gates-for-ll.md); [card 2241](https://observer.anoptic.io/cards/2241-humanize-judgement-engineering-for-agentic-coding.md)). The next gains therefore come from widening what that judge can see rather than adding judges: what a session read, what the operator approved, what a quote says, which skills a listing names. Each of the five candidates is a new input to the same kernel, which is why each fits the line bound and the one-implementation rule where every imported engine fails them.

What orders everything after the five is hireme's own data, which neither planset had: how many CV rules the operator writes, how many score overrides and application outcomes exist, how often agent retries duplicate, and what BOOT is made of. The harness and a few counts turn the remaining estimates into decisions. The job-market evidence also reframes what hireme is for. When customization stops signaling and LLM screeners flip on presentation, the durable advantages are choosing where to apply and making every claim checkable, which FIRE HOLD, the heat governor and cited provenance already encode, so any generation feature should be judged by whether it makes claims more verifiable, not more fluent.
